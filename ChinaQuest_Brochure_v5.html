Where Students Go

Four Core Modules Across Modern China

Each city module offers a distinct lens on China · typically 5 days · combinable into longer programmes

Beijing

Roots, Identity, and Living Heritage

Great Wall, imperial landmarks, and the continuity of Chinese civilisation. Hutong observation, calligraphy, and everyday cultural practice.

A module focused on how history, memory, and identity shape modern China

Shanghai

Global Interface, Exchange, and Modern Chinese Identity

The Bund, Pudong, and the story of urban transformation. School exchange, peer dialogue, and neighbourhood discovery.

A module focused on global connection, cultural fusion, and city life in contemporary China

Shenzhen

Future Ecosystems, Innovation, and STEM Exploration

Technology ecosystems, robotics, and hands-on innovation exposure. Smart mobility, urban change, and future city systems.

A module focused on how design, making, and innovation shape the world students are growing into

Hong Kong

Crossroads, Sustainability, and Global Outlook

Harbour, density, and East–West urban histories. Field-based sustainability learning and local community observation.

A module focused on comparison, reflection, and bringing the wider journey into sharper perspective

Additional city modules (Hangzhou, Chengdu, Xi'an) available on request.

Frequently Asked Questions

What ages is China Quest suitable for?
China Quest is designed for students aged 14–18. Programme content, supervision, and activities are tailored to this age group, with a balance of challenge, support, and structured learning.
What is a city module?
A city module is a structured part of the programme built around a specific learning lens, such as history, globalisation, innovation, or sustainability. Each module has its own theme, duration, and set of core experiences, and modules can be combined to create a broader journey.
How long are the programmes?
Programmes are built from city-based learning modules. Core city modules are typically 5 days and can be combined into longer programmes depending on your school's goals, student group, and schedule.
Can schools customise the itinerary?
Yes. Schools can select and combine modules, adjust the overall programme length, and discuss specific themes or priorities. We work with each school to shape a programme that fits its students, goals, and schedule.
Can a teacher accompany the group?
Yes. Schools are welcome to nominate a teacher to accompany the group. A travelling teacher can help support continuity, pastoral care, reflection, and alignment with school expectations throughout the programme.
Who runs China Quest?
China Quest is run by Miles Minds Limited, an Ireland-based education company with a team across China. It is delivered with local support, bilingual leadership, and a strong focus on school-facing programme design.
Do students need to speak Chinese?
No. All programme leaders are bilingual, and activities are delivered in English. Students may naturally pick up a few basic phrases along the way, but no prior Chinese language knowledge is required.
Is China safe for student groups?
Yes. China Quest is designed with student wellbeing, clear supervision, and risk-aware planning in mind. Programmes include experienced bilingual leaders, a maximum 1:8 leader-to-student ratio, defined emergency procedures, and international travel and health insurance.
What happens in an emergency?
Each programme includes clearly defined emergency procedures, including immediate medical access, direct communication channels, and practical travel adjustments where needed. Detailed emergency and supervision information is available in the School Pack.
What is included in the programme fee?
Programme fees typically include accommodation, meals, domestic transport, entry fees, core activities, insurance, learning materials, and 24/7 bilingual support. International flights, visa fees, and personal spending are not included. Full details are available in the School Pack.
Is the programme Transition Year compatible?
Yes. China Quest is designed with Transition Year learning in mind, including reflection tasks, portfolio-style outputs, and opportunities for collaboration, autonomy, and critical thinking.
Can China Quest support Gaisce?
Some elements of the programme may support TY portfolio work and may also align with aspects of Gaisce, depending on each student's goals and school or PAL guidance. We recommend discussing this with your school in advance.
